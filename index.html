About the workshop

Quantum dynamics meets scientific computing

We are happy to announce our workshop on Numerical Methods in Quantum Spin Dynamics, taking place on 14 October 2026 in Uppsala.

The goal of the workshop is to bring together researchers working at the interface of quantum spin dynamics, open quantum systems, mathematical modeling, and scientific computing. The morning is intended to provide a setting for expert discussions on recent physical models, structure-preserving formulations, tensor techniques, and scalable numerical methods for increasingly large quantum systems.

The workshop is hosted as a satellite to the Swedish e-Science Academy. Participants associated with any of the three eSSENCE partner universities Umeå, Lund, and Uppsala will have their accommodation covered through eSSENCE.

Talks & abstracts

Talk 1

A high-temperature limit penalizing high-frequency quantum fluctuations

Erik Aurell · KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm, Sweden

The time evolution of a quantum system interacting with an Ohmic bath of harmonic oscillators is one of the most widely studied models of open quantum systems theory. It applies to many physical situations in which damping becomes friction-like, or frequency independent, in the semiclassical limit, including photon and phonon baths and superconducting qubits coupled to resistive elements in quantum circuits.

Caldeira and Leggett famously used this model to investigate the classical limit of quantum Brownian motion and macroscopic tunnelling. An important conceptual difficulty is that a straightforward high-temperature limit leads to a quantum Markov evolution that is not a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map. In the language of Lindblad equations, one of the jump rates is slightly negative, and the evolution is therefore not governed by a proper Lindblad equation.

In this talk, the model will be revisited and an additional contribution to the decoherence kernel will be derived in a new high-temperature limit at arbitrarily large cut-off frequency. The resulting evolution is a Lindblad equation with positive jump rates. The additional term reveals a mechanism for the classicalization of high-frequency quantum fluctuations through damping. End contributions to the Feynman–Vernon kernel and the opposite low-temperature regime, where long-time memory becomes important, will also be discussed.

Based on joint work with Graeme Pleasance and Francesco Petruccione, Physical Review Letters 137(5), 050405 (2026).

Talk 2

Nonlinear dissipative quantum spin dynamics and their numerical solutions

Vahid Azimi-Mousolou & Davoud Mirzaei · Uppsala University, Uppsala, Sweden

In the first part of the talk, we discuss the quantum formulation of nonlinear dissipative spin dynamics. In particular, we introduce a quantum analogue of the Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert equation, discuss its geometric foundations and generalizations, and examine its physical properties and potential applications in quantum technologies.

In the second part, we present a numerical method, called low-rank eigenmode integration (LREI), for simulating these dynamics in the mixed-state regime, where exact solutions are generally not available. LREI uses the low-rank structure of the density matrix together with the sparsity of the Hamiltonian to substantially reduce memory requirements and computational complexity, enabling simulations with millions of degrees of freedom. At the same time, the method preserves the physical properties of the underlying models.
